The streaming event grammar

Every streaming response in pi — from any of a thousand models — is narrated in one event language. You've consumed it twice already (in the agent loop, and indirectly on screen). Now read the full grammar, and learn the TypeScript idiom that makes consuming it safe: exhaustive narrowing.

The union, complete

Twelve shapes, but really a 3×3 grid plus bookends:

  • Three content kinds (text, thinking, toolcall) × three phases (start, delta, end). A block opens at some contentIndex in the message's content array, grows by deltas, and closes — _end carrying the assembled whole (note toolcall_end delivers a finished, parsed ToolCall).
  • One opener, start, when the provider accepts the request.
  • Two closers, exactly one of which ends every stream: done or error. Look at the closers' types: Extract<StopReason, "stop" | "length" | "toolUse"> means "the subset of StopReason that indicates success" — the type system enforcing that a done event can't claim it ended in an error, and vice versa.

And the design decision you already know from lesson 2.4: every event carries partial, the full message-so-far. Consumers that care about fine structure use the deltas; consumers that just want "the current state of the reply" read partial and stay trivially correct.

TypeScript · switch narrowing and exhaustiveness

Here is the full power of a discriminated union when you consume it:

Inside each case, the compiler narrows event to that single shape — fields from other shapes don't exist. The default trick is the pro move: if every case is handled, event has type never ("nothing can reach here") and the assignment compiles. Add a thirteenth event to the union next year, and every switch written this way fails to compile until someone handles it. Python's match plus mypy can approximate this; in this codebase it's the standard safety net around every union you've met.

One grammar, many dialects

Here's why this union earns its keep. Anthropic's wire protocol sends content_block_start / content_block_delta; OpenAI sends choices[0].delta fragments; Google sends candidate chunks. Every adapter's whole job (next module) is translating its dialect into this grammar. Above the adapter line, nobody — not the loop, not the UI, not your extension — ever sees a provider event again. When you hear "unified streaming API," this union is the unification.

Note · events vs. the Agent's events

Keep the two unions straight: AssistantMessageEvent (this lesson) narrates one response being generated; AgentEvent (lesson 2.2) narrates a whole agent run — turns, tools, lifecycle. The loop consumes the first and produces the second; you watched it do so, case by case, in lesson 2.4's switch statement.
